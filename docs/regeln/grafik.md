# Rules: rendering, materials, textures, particles

Part of the agent manual (`CLAUDE.md`). Read this before you change the renderer, a TSL material, post-processing, textures or particles.

- The tag at the end of a rule is the session that wrote it; rules written after Session 20 carry their date. **If two rules disagree, the later one wins** (date > `S20 …` > `S19` > … > `≤ S9`).
- Design intent: `DESIGN.md` → „Grafik & Effekte". Sky, fog, rain and lights: `umwelt.md`. Pod materials and the shared Gegner pipelines: also `pods.md`.

## Renderer and TSL
- Renderer/node classes from `three/webgpu`, TSL from `three/tsl`. **No `ShaderMaterial` / `onBeforeCompile` / `EffectComposer`.** *(≤ S9)*
- WGSL strictness: no constant `smoothstep(a,b,x)` with `a >= b` → `ssInv`. Keep all `.sample()` calls in uniform control flow; inside `If`/`Loop` use `textureLoad(...)` (for depth) or `.level(0)` / `.grad(dx, dy)`. In `Fn((_params, builder) => ...)`, `builder` is the **2nd** argument. *(≤ S9)*
- **Pattern on an instanced mesh: `positionGeometry`, not `positionLocal`.** On an `InstancedMesh`, `positionLocal` is already multiplied by the instance matrix (metres in the mesh's space, for a world-space instance: world metres). A pattern built on it scales with the instance and wanders with its position – the heat shield came out as a black disc. `positionGeometry` is the untouched vertex. *(2026-11-23)*
- **Additive light reads far stronger than its number.** 0.14 linear blue on dark basalt is already a medium blue on screen (≈ 105 / 255). Fill terms of additive effects stay near 0.01 … 0.1; lines and rims carry the look. *(2026-11-23)*
- **`screenUV` origin is top-left on WebGPU *and* WebGL2.** Any NDC → screen conversion must flip y (`0.5 - ndc.y * 0.5`). The old god-ray code did not. *(S11)*

## Normals, materials, batching
- **Normals & displacement:** Terrain/water/lava use singularity-free, derivative-free `xzNormalToView` / `transformNormalToView` + `polygonOffset` (`terrainMaterial.ts`). Roads/cliffs/pods use 128× pre-scaled derivative TBN with silhouette-jump rejection (`safePerturbNormal` / `safeHullNormal`). Never apply vertex `displace` to `wallMat` (tears seams); on `roadMat` taper displacement to `0` at `uv.x = 0/1`. Build PMREM with `makeSky(theme, true)`. *(≤ S9)*
- **Static batching, pods & particles:** `StaticBatcher` (`trackMesh.ts`) merges world-space chunks into 580 m `THREE.Mesh` tiles (no `BatchedMesh`). All 10 opponent pods share **3 single TSL pipelines** (`sharedOpponentHullMaterial`, `sharedOpponentFlameMaterial`, `sharedOpponentBinderMaterial`) via baked vertex colours + 2-tier LOD (`48 m` / `520 m`). `PointsNodeMaterial` (`particles.ts`) clamps `sizeNode` to `≤ 0.16 * -positionView.z` and `0` for `vz < 1.2 m` with near-fade (prevents camera-plane overdraw & keeps sightlines clear). Texture budget: max 16/stage (peak 12–13/16; height in `map.a`, roughness in `normalMap.a`). *(≤ S9)*
- **Blended ground – `surfaceMaterial(…, { fade: 'soft' })` (`materials.ts`):** the vertex attribute `aFade` (0 … 1) is turned into alpha against a noise threshold that is low in the hollows of the surface's own height map; the rim of every patch is `SOFT` = 0.38 wide. Used for everything that lets one ground run out into another: the stretch where a natural passage fades in over the previous one, the shoulder of a natural road, the apron of a deck at a lake, the sand / snow drifts. Rules: (1) the material is `transparent` WITH `depthWrite` and `alphaTest` 0.012 – it is ground, later particles and glass must be tested against it; (2) it must be drawn right after the opaque pass and before every other transparent object: pass `GROUND_ORDER.BODY` (shoulders) or `GROUND_ORDER.OVER` (anything lying on another ground, drifts) as the last argument of `StaticBatcher.addStrip` – the batcher keeps one group per (material, order); `OVER` after `BODY`, or the body is depth-rejected under the faint rim of the overlay; (3) `userData.ground` makes it count as opaque in the SSR buffers (`post.ts`, `opaqueAlpha`); (4) every natural surface has two materials, `surfMat` (opaque, the road itself) and `surfBlend` – never give the solid road the blend material (transparent pass for the whole road). Before 2026-11-23 the same attribute drove a hard noise dissolve with `alphaTest` 0.5 (`fade: true`, still there): transitions were cut-out blotches. *(2026-11-23)*
- **Wall sheets (`world.ts`, canyon walls): the texture's v runs with the LENGTH of the wall profile, not with its height.** The upper benches of `cliffProf` lean back up to 4.6 m per 2 m of height and the cap runs level: measured by height the picture was stretched up to 2.5 times there (the smeared band along every rim, the cap looked like a cloth). The length is taken on the profile WITHOUT crags and ribs – they change from one cross-section to the next and the bedding of the picture would wobble along the wall. **Cap:** where the land behind a wall is within 3.5 m of its rim, the outer half of the cap lies on the drawn terrain and thins out into it (material `cliffBlend` = the wall material with `fade: 'soft'`); a wall that stands free keeps its solid back down into the ground (without it the wall is a paper-thin sail from behind). *(2026-11-23)*
- **Terrain: rock by steepness reads `aSplat.w`, not the vertex normal alone** (`terrain.ts → terrainLeafMaker`, `terrainMaterial.ts → wK`; 2026-11-23). `aSplat.w` = the slope (1 − normal.y) of the STEEPEST of the six faces that meet in the vertex. The vertex normal is a central difference: at the foot and at the rim of a low cliff it is flat although the faces beside it are steep (measured on Boonta: face normal.y 0.37–0.52, vertex normal.y 0.40 / 0.47 / **0.98** in one triangle), so steep faces ran out into sand at such corners – a row of rock teeth, one per cell, along every low cliff. Now a steep face is rock in all three corners; the change to sand, snow or grass lies on the flat ground beside it and is frayed by the photo's height map. Unit check `terrain: every face …` compares the weight with the real triangles of the leaf mesh (red if the split of the cells and the formula ever drift apart). The fourth channel held an unused random number before – no extra memory, no extra height samples.
  - Not done (still `TODO.md` section 8): the thresholds and widths of the other layers (`wG` gravel by splat, `wB` basin), snow on ledges by height, basin shores.
- **Rock that GREW never takes the wall texture of a world whose wall is BUILT** (`hasBuiltWall` in `data.ts`: Aquilaris – metal plates with hazard marks; Oovo IV – station plating with lights; 2026-11-23). Until then everything "rock" used `wallTex` / `wallMat`: on Aquilaris the steep flanks of sand dunes, the boulders, rock rows and arches were plated with hazard-marked metal, on Oovo IV the rock rows and asteroids were lumps of station plating. Now `world.ts` has two names and every call site picks one:
  - `wallTex` / `wallMat` (material name `cliff`): what was built – wall sheets, skirts, tunnels, bridge piers, basin rims.
  - `rockTex` / `rockMat` (on the two built worlds a material of its own, name `rock`; elsewhere the same object as `wallMat`): what grew – border rocks, decor rocks, arches, obstacle rocks, set-piece rock, landmark rock, the asteroid belt, ground-cover stones. The terrain's steep slopes use `cliffTex`, the same set.
  - A world with a built wall always gets that rock set (`worldTextureSpecs`; colours from `TerrainLayers.cliff`, grey if it names none). **A new theme with a man-made wall kind must be added to `hasBuiltWall`.**
  - Tunnels of a built wall keep the vault's UVs (material `tunnelWall` = the wall material, double-sided): panels follow the tube. The world-space projection of `caveMaterial` below is for rock only.
  - Left as designed: the wall SHEETS of Aquilaris are metal, their tops tinted moss green by the ledge colour – from 60 m above that is a wide green tiled band, from the road a plated wall with a green rim.
- **Rock INSIDE tunnels and caves is `caveMaterial` (`materials.ts`), not `surfaceMaterial`** (2026-11-23; meshes: vault `shell` / `caveShell`, cave shelves and wedge floors, end walls, stalactites and stalagmites; material names `caveRock`, `caveRockFlat`). Two reasons:
- **Decoration that belongs to an open gorge asks for the roof:** `trackDetail.ts` strings catenary cables with a beacon across every stretch with walls of 18 m and more on both sides – a tunnel has such walls too, so until 2026-11-23 cables crossed the tube, wider than the vault, their ends in the rock (a thin dark line across the vault of the Boonta tunnel). Now `if (s.roof > 0) continue` – AFTER the random numbers of the cable are drawn: `addTrackDetail` shares one random stream, a skipped draw would move everything placed after it.
  1. **Projection.** The vault's UVs run along the tunnel and around the arch. Mapped by UV, the layering of the rock followed the arch like the courses of a brick vault, and wherever a cave widens the rings have different lengths, so the texture sheared into swirls. `caveMaterial` projects the walls' photo set in WORLD space from the three axes and blends by the surface normal (15 m per repeat, weights `|n|⁴`): layers stay level on the walls, the roof shows the rock from below, nothing stretches, branch tubes and shelves continue the same rock. The normal maps of the three projections add up to one world-space tilt of the normal (u and v of each projection are world axes). Three colour and three normal reads plus the detail copy, **no parallax loop** – not more expensive than before. What remains and is correct: level layers cut by a barrel vault are lines along the tunnel, and they follow every bulge of a cave like contour lines.
  2. **Light.** Nothing but the hemisphere light and the sky's environment map reach into a tunnel – on a roof that is the theme's GROUND colour. In the lava world it is almost black: lamps and glow veins were drawn but lit nothing, the cave was a black hole with orange dots. Their light is **baked per vertex** in `buildTunnel → litAt` (attribute `aLit`): a patch around every roof lamp (6 m), a broad wash (38 m), the glow veins along the wall feet of a cave. The material adds `rock colour × lamp colour × aLit`, shaded by the relief as if lit from above. It costs no light source. **The amount depends on the world** (`CAVE_LIGHT = [1.5, 0.12]`, blended by the luminance of `hemiGround`): lava, moon, crystal get the full amount; from jungle brightness upwards the lamps only add a little – with the dark amount the jungle cave glowed lime green, three to five times brighter than the land outside.
  - **Not covered:** the ROAD and the pods inside a tunnel get no lamp light (the road's materials are shared with the open track). On the black basalt of the lava cave the road stays black between its edge lines. A real light there would cost every pixel of every race; baking `aLit` into the road needs a second set of road materials. In races that have lights at all (`EnvBuild.lights`: weather or time of day can get dark) the pods switch their lamps on under a roof (`Game.updateLights`: `max(lightLevel, cover)`).
  - The rock seen from OUTSIDE (berms, headwalls: `m2` in `buildTunnel`) stays `surfaceMaterial`.
- **Drifts (`trackDetail.ts`):** sand (desert, mesa) and snow (ice) blown against rails, rocks and cliffs are strips of that world's loose ground in its blend material (`DetailOpts.drift`, world-space UVs, `aFade` falling from the wall inwards and with the strength of the drift along the track). None on passages that are that ground themselves (`drift.same`). Without `drift` the function builds the opaque band of before. *(2026-11-23)*
- **Snow layer (`snowDeck.ts`) – what the first version got wrong:** (1) tangent × right points DOWN in this code base (`right = (−cos yaw, sin yaw)`): a grid built along / across the track needs the index order `i0, i1, i2` or it is back-face culled and simply invisible. (2) A texture read in `positionNode` must be `.level(0)` (vertex stage); the fragment stage takes its own `texture()` nodes. (3) One-channel data: `DataTexture(…, RedFormat, UnsignedByteType)`, linear filter, no mipmaps, `needsUpdate` only when the field is dirty. Checked on WebGL2 (pictures) and WebGPU (`check:gpu`, track 1). *(2026-11-22)*

## Post-processing
- **The warm-up draws `WARM_FRAMES` (4) frames, not one** (`post.ts → warmup`): a pass that is a node of its own (SSR, bloom, the RTT textures between two passes) builds its inner materials again in the frame AFTER the material round it was built – their `setup()` sets `needsUpdate`, the old render object is disposed and its pipeline compiled anew. With SSR + SMAA the chain is three frames long: with two warm-up frames the three SSR pipelines (`SSR [ Reflections ]`, `Blur Level 0 / 1`) were compiled in the first frame of the countdown. After adding a pass that contains other passes, repeat the proof in `tests.md` ("no pipeline is compiled after the warm-up"). *(2026-11-22)*
- **Heat haze slots:** `post.heat.plumes` has 6 slots (player + 2 nearest rivals). *(S19)*
- **`reflRes`** (0.35–1) scales the SSR buffer; restart-only like SSR. *(S17)*
- **SSR:** MRT channel `metalrough.r` is *reflectivity* (`max(metalness, smooth-dielectric term)`); SSRNode discards pixels where it is 0. New glossy non-metal surfaces need roughness < 0.3 to reflect. *(S15)*
- **Heat haze:** `post.heat` (2 screen positions, radius, strength), set in `Game.render`. UV offset only – keep it free of extra samples and control flow. *(S12)*
- **Lens effects (`post.ts`):** compiled in only when needed (`env.rain > 0.05`, `env.flare`), gated by `gfx.lensFx`. Flare peak add < 0.85; do not raise (spec: no overexposure). Lightning must not use the full-screen `flash` uniform. *(S11)*
- **PostFX & warmup (`post.ts`):** AO = 4-pair symmetric `textureLoad` depth concavity (0 MRT, 0 flat-plane striping, 0 halos, immune to transparents). Bloom runs at `0.5×` resolution (`threshold = 2.1`). God rays = 14-tap `textureLoad` sky-depth shafts. SSR (opt-in) masks transparents with compile-time `alpha = 0` + `NormalBlending` in MRT. Always run `await post.warmup(renderer)` (with seeded particles & revealed pod LODs) before `onReady()` so countdown frame 1 never stalls. Never use full-screen CSS `filter: drop-shadow` on HUD text (`textShadow` only). *(≤ S9)*

## Graphics settings: live or restart
- **Live graphics now also:** volumetrics (`fog.hOn` / `fog.bankOn` uniforms – the fog branch is always compiled) and anisotropy (`setTextureAnisotropy`). CSM and SSR stay restart-only. *(S16)*
- **Live graphics:** `Game.LIVE_GFX` lists what `applyLiveGfx` can change at runtime; `POST_KEYS` trigger a rebuild of `PostFX` (dispose old, warm up new). Anything baked into geometry/compiled materials stays restart-only – keep the two UI texts (pause menu, README) in sync with the list. *(S15)*
- **CSM** is opt-in (`gfx.csm`): `CSMShadowNode` on `sun.shadow.shadowNode`; call `csm.updateFrustums()` only after `csm.camera` exists (first render). *(S10)*

## Textures
- **PBR photo sets (`pbrTex.ts`, `photoAssets.ts`, `src/assets/pbr/<name>_{c,n,p}.jpg`, lava also `_e`):** `TexSpec.photo = '<set>'` replaces the procedural bytes when the set is registered (browser only – node CI and `?nophoto` fall back to the spec's `kind`). Packed into the usual TexSet layout (height in `map.a`, roughness in `normalMap.a`), albedo graded to the spec's palette. New set → entry in `tools/photos/pbr.py` (CC0 sources only), import in `photoAssets.ts`, credit in README. 1024 px JPEG, tileable. *(S20 Strecken)*
- **Texture memory:** `beginTextureEpoch()` (world build) keeps only the current and the previous world's sets. Do not cache TexSets at module level outside `textures.ts`. *(S20 Strecken)*
- **Photo textures:** `photoTex.ts` (`KIND` map, `applyPhotoDetail` in `textures.wrap()` on COPIES – the persistent cache stores the pure procedural bytes), sources bundled in `photoAssets.ts`, registered by `main.tsx` and the harness only. Photos must be 512² power-of-two, tileable, lighting-flattened (`tools/photos/process.mjs`). Any esbuild call that bundles `Game` needs `--loader:.jpg=dataurl`. *(S18)*
- **Texture cache:** `texStore.ts` (IndexedDB), enabled only by `main.tsx`. Keys include a hash of the synthesis source – helper functions used by `computeTexture` must be part of that hash (`storeKey` in `textures.ts`) or stale textures survive code changes. *(S14)*
- **Textures:** `texCore.ts` must stay free of three.js/DOM imports (it is bundled into the worker). `generateTexture` = sync, `generateTextures(specs)` = worker pool with in-flight dedupe; `worldTextureSpecs` is the single list used by the pre-warm in `Game.start` and by `buildWorld`. Worker registration only in `main.tsx` (`?worker&inline`) – harness and node stay synchronous. *(S13)*
- `rock` texture: never re-add un-warped Worley block cracks (reads as brick wall). *(S9)*

- **Ground-cover plants with a drawn outline (`groundCover.ts`, first: the fern, 2026-11-23):** every frond is ONE card (four segments, u across, v from the stalk to the tip); stalk and leaflets come from a drawn texture (`frondTextures`, 128 × 512). Pattern to follow for the next plant: (1) colour and alpha are two canvases, the colour canvas is leaf green all over – no dark fringe in the smaller mip levels (same as the tree atlas in `foliage.ts`); (2) the geometry keeps its `uv` (`part(…, keepUv)`), the vertex colour only shades; (3) the alpha goes through a STEEP step (`smoothstep(0.25, 0.55, …)`) before it meets the distance dither of `coverMaterial` – far away the mip levels hold 0.5 … 0.7 over the whole frond, read as is the dither thins the plant out to a haze; (4) give the geometry a `name`, the harness view `g_<name>[_<instance>[_<distance × 10>]]` then shows it. Triangle count as before (96 instead of 90 per fern).
- **Ground-cover stones and pebbles carry the rock of the world's walls** (`groundCover.ts → coverMaterial`, `rock`; 2026-11-23). They have no UVs, so the photo set is projected in world space from the three axes (`STONE_TILE` = 3.5 m per repeat; the walls use 15 m). Before, flat vertex colours made them pale lumps beside the photo-textured rock rows and spires – a desaturated brown in the shade under a blue sky renders light grey-blue, it looked like a missing texture. The vertex colour stays as a tint (divided by the stone's own mean colour: facet variation, white snow caps, half the hue of the piece – with the full hue the pebbles on orange sandstone turned signal orange). A new stone kind gets it by building its `Built` with `rockOf(colour, …)`. Bushes and shrubs are still flat-coloured lumps (`TODO.md` section 5).

## Particles and dust
- **No baked marks on natural ground (2026-11-23).** `props.ts` laid ≈ 110 „skid mark" decals per track on the road: ten thin, sharp, dark lines each – tyre tracks on a track for hovering pods, dark on white on the snow passages (user: „Streifen wie Reifenspuren, besonders auf Schnee"). They are turbine scorch now – two broad, soft, speckled soot streaks – and lie only on the built deck (`surf === 0`). Marks on natural ground come from the pods themselves (snow trails; sand and moon dust: `TODO.md` Abschnitt 9). The placement still draws its random numbers for every candidate, so everything placed after it stays where it was.
- **Particle pools add update ranges** (the renderer clears them after upload). Any test that runs several `Game.update()` calls without rendering must call `pool.fullUpload()` before the screenshot (the harness does) – otherwise particles are missing in the picture although the game is fine. *(S17)*
- **Own-pod dust must stay visible:** spawn under the engines, inherit ~80 % of the pod velocity with strong drag (slipstream), slight outward drift; alpha-pool near fade is 3–8.5 m. Check with harness view `r`. *(S17)*
- **Dust:** `particles.trail` (dry) / `particles.spray` (wet) from both engine tails in `Game.updateVisuals`; `dustAmt` scales it per world. Keep the 24 m near-camera reduction for rivals. *(S15)*
- **Particles (`particles.ts`):** stateless GPU simulation. `Pool.spawn` writes one record (5 × vec4) into a ring buffer; the vertex shader integrates drag/gravity in closed form. No per-frame CPU loop – never reintroduce `pos/vel` arrays. Colour/alpha travel as one `varying(vec4)`. *(S10)*

## Performance target
- **Target hardware:** "Ultra" aims at a gaming PC (Core i9 + RTX 4090) – max detail there; lower presets must not get slower. The dev laptop cannot prove Ultra frame rate: report triangles + draw calls (ADR 0002). *(S20 Pods)*
